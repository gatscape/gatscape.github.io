I want to build a service that quickly advises people on whether a lifestyle habit is time tested or not. 

This isn't about the "evidence based" Science du Jour or general public literature reviews which examine.com does nicely, but about:

* What has been filtered over hundreds of years (empirically, devoid of human reason). 
* What habits are cross culture or monoculture (establishing universality).
* No expertimental/lab studies, since it's often error prone generalising from labs since the conditions these results were obtained in isn't always what people are in real life.

Influencers are giving damaaging advice: A lot of the online Longevity and Health influcners are likely doing harm to their customers. Creatine has no long term studies looking at health consequences, yet the major influencers are selling it as a _riskless product_. No down side? If creatine is a magic supplement, why didn't our bodies naturally produce more of it? A personal trainer recently told me it was "well researched", what does "well researched" even mean?

We focus on the harm and the via-negativa, not the confirmation. What is wrong, not what is right. The site must be in the spirit of skeptical empiricism. We primarily want to provide evidence that something isn't safe, since the assumption is that unknown downside is not worth whatever the gain is. 

We don't have answers to these questions. Therefore - invoking the precautionary principle - it is not worth it. When the gains are small, and loss unknown, go with the safe option, as a rule.

This is what <<lindyhealth>> is all about.
